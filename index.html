The problem

Your decisions already exist. You just can't find them.

They're scattered across Slack threads that scroll away, Notion pages nobody reopens, and the one person who happens to remember — until they don't, or until they leave.

Buried in chat

The reasoning lives in a thread from four months ago that nobody can search for, because nobody remembers the exact words used.

Written once, read never

Someone writes a beautiful Notion doc. Three weeks later nobody remembers it exists — so the same debate happens again.

Tribal memory walks out the door

The "why" behind a decision lives in one person's head. When they're out sick, on leave, or leave the company — it's gone.

How it works

Three steps. No new habit to build.

Cairn isn't another tool your team has to remember to open. It fits in the gaps between the tools you already use.

1

Log the decision

Title, context, and the actual reasoning — 30 seconds after you decide something, while it's still fresh.

2

It joins your team's memory

Every decision is tagged, timestamped, and instantly searchable by anyone on the team — forever.

3

Ask Cairn anything

"Why did we drop feature X?" Cairn reads your team's whole decision history and answers in plain language, with sources.

Features

Small enough to actually use daily.

No workflow to learn, no admin console to configure. Built for teams of two people, and it still holds up at twenty.

Ask Cairn

Type a question in plain English. Cairn searches your team's entire decision history and answers with the reasoning — plus links to the source decisions it used.

Instant capture

One shortcut, one form. Log a decision faster than you could type a Slack message about it.

Tags & categories

Product, pricing, hiring, ops — organize decisions your way, and filter your whole history in one click.

Clear timeline

Scroll through your company's own history, month by month. It's oddly satisfying to watch it grow.

Your data, your workspace

No public sharing, no scraping your Slack. A private workspace your team joins with an invite code — nothing more.
